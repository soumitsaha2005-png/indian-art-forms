Heritage & Legacy

A Civilization Painted in a Thousand Hues

India's artistic traditions span over 5,000 years, weaving together the sacred and the secular, the royal and the rural. From the cave paintings of Ajanta to the vibrant folk traditions of Bengal's Patuas, each art form carries the DNA of its region — its climate, its mythology, and its people.

This exhibition brings together the most significant traditional art forms from across the Indian subcontinent, with special focus on the rich artistic legacies of West Bengal and Tamil Nadu alongside treasures from Rajasthan, Maharashtra, Odisha, Bihar, and Madhya Pradesh.

Art Forms of India

Discover masterpieces from every corner of the subcontinent — each carrying the soul of its land.

West Bengal

Kalighat Painting

Kolkata, West Bengal

Born near the Kalighat temple in 19th-century Kolkata, this art form features bold, sweeping brushstrokes and simplified, powerful forms. Originally depicting Hindu deities, it evolved into sharp social commentary — making it India's earliest form of visual satire.

Folk Art 19th Century Watercolor
Tamil Nadu

Tanjore Painting

Thanjavur, Tamil Nadu

Celebrated for their opulence, Tanjore paintings use gold foil, semi-precious stones, and rich, jewel-toned colours to depict Hindu deities. Originating during the Maratha rule of Thanjavur, they exemplify South India's devotion crystallized in art.

Classical Gold Leaf 16th Century
Bihar

Madhubani Painting

Mithila, Bihar

Practised for over 2,500 years by the women of Mithila, Madhubani art transforms walls and floors into living mythologies. Its geometric precision, vibrant natural pigments, and symbolic motifs — fish, peacocks, lotus — narrate stories of fertility, devotion, and cosmic harmony.

Folk Art 2500+ Years Natural Dyes
Maharashtra

Warli Painting

Thane District, Maharashtra

One of the oldest art forms on Earth, Warli painting uses only white rice paste on ochre mud walls. Its minimalist geometric vocabulary — circles, triangles, and lines — captures the rhythms of tribal life: harvest dances, hunts, wedding processions, and the eternal bond with nature.

Tribal Art 3000+ Years Geometric
Odisha

Pattachitra

Raghurajpur, Odisha

Painted on specially prepared cotton cloth with natural colors, Pattachitra is a visual scripture. Devotional narratives unfold panel by panel — Jagannath, Ramayana, Mahabharata — rendered with extraordinary line-control and ornate floral borders that frame each sacred story.

Devotional 12th Century Cloth Painting
Tamil Nadu

Kolam

Tamil Nadu & South India

Drawn at dawn with rice flour on freshly washed thresholds, Kolam is a daily mathematical meditation. Its infinite loops and fractal symmetries welcome prosperity while feeding ants and insects — a perfect union of aesthetics, geometry, ecology, and devotion that is recreated fresh each morning.

Floor Art Ephemeral Geometric
West Bengal

Patachitra (Bengal Scroll)

Midnapore, West Bengal

The Patua storytellers of Bengal unroll long vertical scrolls while singing narrative ballads — a living tradition merging visual art, music, and theatre. These scrolls once carried Mangalkavya epics; today they tackle climate change, social justice, and pandemic awareness with the same vivid visual grammar.

Scroll Art Performing Art Narrative
Tamil Nadu

Chola Bronze Sculpture

Thanjavur & Kumbakonam, Tamil Nadu

The lost-wax bronze casting of the Chola dynasty (9th–13th century) produced what many consider the pinnacle of Indian sculpture. The iconic Nataraja — Shiva as cosmic dancer — is recognized worldwide as a masterpiece of metallurgy and spiritual expression, embodying creation and destruction in a single form.

Sculpture 9th Century Bronze
Rajasthan

Rajasthani Miniature

Jaipur, Jodhpur & Udaipur, Rajasthan

Patronized by Rajput courts, these exquisite miniatures capture royal life, divine love stories of Radha-Krishna, and Rajasthan's majestic landscapes in astonishing detail. Using brushes made from single squirrel hairs, artists created worlds of jewel-like precision on surfaces no larger than a palm.

Miniature 16th Century Court Art
Maharashtra

Ajanta Cave Murals

Aurangabad, Maharashtra

Hidden in a horseshoe-shaped gorge, the 30 rock-cut caves of Ajanta preserve some of humanity's greatest paintings — Buddhist Jataka tales rendered in earth pigments over 2,000 years ago. Their mastery of chiaroscuro, perspective, and emotional expression predates Renaissance techniques by a millennium.

Mural 2nd Century BCE UNESCO
West Bengal

Dokra Metal Craft

Bankura & Purulia, West Bengal

Using the lost-wax (cire perdue) technique unchanged for 4,000 years, the Dokra artisans of Bengal create hauntingly beautiful tribal figurines, horses, elephants, and deities. Each piece is unique — the mould is destroyed in the casting, making every sculpture a one-of-a-kind treasure.

Metal Craft 4000 Years Lost-Wax
Madhya Pradesh

Gond Painting

Mandla & Dindori, Madhya Pradesh

The Gond community transforms their deep connection with forests into mesmerizing dot-and-dash compositions. Trees, birds, tigers, and myths are rendered in intricate patterns that reveal hidden worlds within worlds — a visual philosophy where every creature holds the memory of the forest.

Tribal Art Indigenous Dot Pattern
West Bengal

Kantha Embroidery

Bengal (West Bengal & Bangladesh)

Born from frugality, Kantha transforms discarded saris into quilted masterpieces through running stitches that create dreamlike scenes — a tree of life, a lotus pond, village processions. Each Kantha is a mother's love stitched into fabric — traditionally gifted to daughters at marriage.

Textile Art Embroidery Sustainable
Rajasthan

Phad Painting

Bhilwara, Rajasthan

Phad is a 700-year-old tradition of narrative scroll painting depicting the heroic deeds of folk deities Pabuji and Devnarayan. Painted on long bolts of cloth, these scrolls serve as portable temples — carried by Bhopa priests who perform all-night recitations illuminated only by lamplight.

Scroll Art Narrative 700 Years
Through the Ages

A Timeline of Indian Art

Key moments that shaped the artistic soul of the subcontinent.

3000 BCE
Warli & Tribal Origins
The earliest roots of Indian art emerge in tribal communities — geometric symbols on cave walls and mud huts that would become the Warli tradition.
2nd Century BCE
Ajanta Cave Paintings
Buddhist monks begin painting the magnificent murals of Ajanta, creating a visual encyclopedia of Jataka tales over the next seven centuries.
9th–13th Century
Chola Bronze Age
The Chola dynasty of Tamil Nadu produces the world's finest bronze sculptures — the Nataraja becomes the ultimate symbol of Indian art.
12th Century
Pattachitra Emerges
The temple town of Puri nurtures a tradition of devotional cloth paintings that will endure for nearly a millennium.
16th–17th Century
Miniature & Tanjore Golden Age
Mughal and Rajput miniature painting flourishes alongside Tanjore's gold-leaf masterworks — two parallel peaks of artistic refinement.
19th Century
Kalighat Revolution
Near Calcutta's Kalighat temple, artists create bold, satirical paintings that bridge folk tradition and modern commentary — India's first popular art movement.
21st Century
Global Renaissance
Indian art forms gain international recognition — Madhubani appears in fashion, Warli in architecture, and Bengal's Patua scrolls address global issues.
